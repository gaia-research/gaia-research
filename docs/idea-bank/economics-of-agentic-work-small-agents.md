## Thesis
The question is not merely whether a problem can be parallelized. It is whether retries, competing small agents, adaptive escalation, and stronger domain tooling can produce a verified result for less total cost than one expensive model, after coordination, latency, integration, quotas, and human attention are counted.

## Essay direction
The founder's first-person voice should be central, not a generic AI-generated essay. Compare (1) single large model, (2) small model with bounded retries, (3) parallel small-model competition, (4) parallel task decomposition, (5) adaptive hedged attempts and escalation. Examine Amdahl's Law, Brooks's coordination problem, distributed-system hedging, economics of verification, FrugalGPT model cascades, and test-time compute allocation. Domain-specific harnesses can change small-agent success probabilities; elaborate Sol-led marathons may spend their advantage on ceremony. Consider $20-tier quota economics, not just API bills.
